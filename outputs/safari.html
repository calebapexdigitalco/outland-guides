3-Day Northern Circuit

3 daysPrivate 4×4Arusha start & finish

Three distinct park environments in a compact safari. It works well as a focused stand-alone trip or a recovery-minded add-on after the mountain.

Landscape
Baobab savannah, a volcanic caldera, and lush groundwater forest.
Best for
Travelers who want a complete northern-circuit snapshot in a short window.
Overnights
Comfortable lodge accommodation is coordinated in the Karatu area.
Day 1

Arusha to Tarangire National Park
Meet your local guide in Arusha around 8:00 AM, then drive roughly three hours toward Tarangire. The afternoon game drive follows baobab-dotted savannah and seasonal swamps, with the Tarangire River drawing elephant, giraffe, zebra, antelope, and predators. Picnic lunch in the park; lodge overnight near Karatu.

Day 2

Ngorongoro Crater
After breakfast, descend into Ngorongoro Crater for a concentrated game drive on the floor of the world’s largest intact volcanic caldera. Expect a rich mix of elephant, buffalo, hippo, hyena, lion, and—when conditions allow—the chance of seeing black rhino. Picnic lunch near the hippo pool; return to the lodge.

Day 3

Lake Manyara to Arusha
Drive to Lake Manyara for a relaxed final game drive through groundwater forest, acacia, and mahogany. Look for buffalo, giraffe, impala, baboon, blue monkey, and the park’s well-known tree-climbing lions. Return to Arusha in the afternoon.

4-Day Northern Circuit

4 daysPrivate 4×4Serengeti overnight

More time in the Serengeti, with the same Tarangire and Ngorongoro foundation. This is the better choice for guests who want a full day dedicated to the plains and their changing animal movement.

Landscape
Nine vegetation zones in Tarangire, crater grassland, and the open Serengeti plains.
Best for
Travelers who want a complete game-drive day in the Serengeti.
Overnights
Lodge accommodation and a Serengeti tented camp are coordinated with the itinerary.
Day 1

Arusha to Tarangire National Park
Leave Arusha after a briefing for a scenic drive to Tarangire. The afternoon game drive explores the river corridors and broad savannah where elephant, zebra, lion, antelope, gerenuk, and birdlife may appear. Picnic lunch in the park; lodge overnight near Karatu.

Day 2

Ngorongoro Crater to Serengeti
Travel through the Ngorongoro Highlands and descend into the crater for a game drive among lion, elephant, buffalo, rhino, leopard, gazelle, reedbuck, hyena, and abundant birdlife. After lunch, continue toward the Serengeti and settle into camp.

Day 3

Full day in Serengeti National Park
Spend the entire day exploring the plains with a packed lunch. Your guide follows conditions, herds, and wildlife behavior rather than forcing a rigid loop. The Serengeti is famous for the great wildebeest migration, but it is compelling in every season.

Day 4

Serengeti to Arusha
Depart after breakfast with lunch packed, driving back through the Ngorongoro Conservation Area toward Arusha. The route carries you from open plains into rolling highlands, with final wildlife and Maasai-country views along the way. Airport transfer can be arranged for suitable evening flights.

Trip readiness

Comfortable days.
Open attention.

You do not need expedition fitness for this safari, but the best experience comes from being ready for early starts, time in the vehicle, uneven ground at lodges and picnic stops, sun exposure, dust, and shifting temperatures. Anyone with mobility, dietary, or medical considerations should tell us early so the local team can plan thoughtfully.

If you are adding safari after Kilimanjaro, build in a recovery night or two. That gives your legs time to settle, protects the transfer day, and lets the first game drive feel like a reset—not another task on a tight itinerary.

Safari gear list

Pack light.
Be ready for the day.

Safari days move between cool mornings, strong afternoon sun, lodge time, and dusty roads. Bring a small, flexible system rather than hard-sided luggage that takes over the vehicle.

On game drive

  • Lightweight long-sleeve shirt and breathable pants
  • Brimmed hat, sunglasses, sunscreen, and lip balm
  • Light insulating layer for early starts and evening air
  • Camera, spare battery, and binoculars if you have them
  • Reusable water bottle and small daypack

In your luggage

  • Comfortable closed-toe shoes or light trail shoes
  • Personal medications, a basic health kit, and insect repellent
  • Travel documents, insurance details, and emergency contacts
  • Neutral-color layers and swimwear if your lodge has a pool
  • Small dry bag or zip bags to protect electronics from dust

We will send a route-specific packing note before you travel, especially if your safari follows Kilimanjaro.

Safari FAQs

Questions before
you go wild.

What is the difference between the 3- and 4-day safari?
The 3-day route pairs Tarangire, Ngorongoro, and Lake Manyara. The 4-day route visits Tarangire and Ngorongoro, then gives you a dedicated full game-drive day in the Serengeti before returning to Arusha. Choose the four-day itinerary when more Serengeti time matters most.
Can I add this after Kilimanjaro?
Yes. It is a natural way to finish a climb. We recommend building in recovery time before the first long driving day, then arranging the lodge, vehicle, park route, and airport plan as one connected itinerary.
Will we see the Big Five or the migration?
Tarangire, Ngorongoro, and the Serengeti create excellent wildlife opportunity, but no ethical safari operator guarantees an animal or a migration crossing. Your local guide follows current conditions and animal movement to make the most of each day.
What type of vehicle and guide are included?
Your route uses a 4×4 safari vehicle and a professional English-speaking local driver-guide. Park planning, scheduled transport, and the named activities are included in the confirmed itinerary.
Where do we stay and what do we eat?
Lodge or camp overnights and itinerary meals are built into your proposal. The standard routes use a lodge in the Karatu area and, on the 4-day route, a Serengeti camp. Your final itinerary confirms the actual properties, meal plan, and any dietary arrangements.
When should I book?
Reach out as soon as you have a rough travel window. More lead time gives us better options for guide schedules, lodging, park routing, flights, and a clean handoff from Kilimanjaro if you are combining the two.
What should I bring?
Bring light layers, sun protection, closed-toe shoes, a reusable bottle, personal medications, camera gear if desired, and a small daypack. See the Gear List tab for the full working list, then use your pre-trip note for seasonal adjustments.
