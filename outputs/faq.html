Expedition FAQ

Know the plan
before you commit.

We will never pretend every mountain, climber, or season is the same. These are solid starting answers; a personal consult turns them into a real plan.

Ask us directly
How do I choose a good tour operator?

Look beyond the lowest price and the prettiest booking page. Ask who actually runs the trip, which local certifications apply, what the guide-to-climber and porter standards are, how altitude and emergencies are handled, and what is included. A good operator is specific, transparent, and comfortable explaining why a route is right for you.

How much does it cost to climb?

Current starting ranges: Kilimanjaro (Machame or Lemosho, 6 or 7 days) from a route-specific quote; Volcán de Fuego / Acatenango from $200; Pico de Orizaba from $2,850 per climber. Final cost depends on route length, private versus small-group service, season, park fees, technical needs, and what you choose to include. International flights, insurance, tips, and personal gear are usually separate.

When is the best time to climb?

The best window depends on the mountain and your priorities. Kilimanjaro is commonly climbed in the drier periods around January–March and June–October. Mexico’s volcano season generally favors the drier, colder months. We will recommend dates around weather, trail conditions, crowds, and your own readiness—not a generic calendar slot.

How long does each expedition take?

Kilimanjaro is offered in six options: Machame 6-Day, Machame 7-Day, Lemosho 6-Day, and Lemosho 7-Day. Volcán de Fuego / Acatenango is commonly a focused 3-day objective. Pico de Orizaba is usually planned over 5 days to allow for arrival, acclimatization, and the summit window. Custom trips are designed around the objective, not squeezed into an arbitrary number of days.

What is the weather like?

Expect changing conditions and big temperature swings. A day can begin warm, turn wet or windy, and finish below freezing at altitude. We build gear lists and daily pacing around the actual terrain and season, then stay conservative when forecasts or conditions call for it.

What gear do I need?

Every objective needs a reliable layering system, weather protection, appropriate footwear, sleep gear, hydration, and a pack that fits. Technical objectives add glacier, helmet, harness, or mountaineering-boot requirements. We review your existing kit before you buy anything. See Gear Help →

How do I train for an expedition?

Build a base of consistent aerobic work, then add climbing-specific elevation gain, loaded carries, leg strength, mobility, and recovery. For technical peaks, add skills practice before travel. The right plan starts with where you are now and progresses gradually; last-minute suffering is not a training strategy.

Is climbing any of your mountains dangerous?

Mountain travel has real risk: altitude, weather, terrain, fatigue, and changing conditions. Risk is managed through the right route, a proper acclimatization schedule, capable guides, medical awareness, emergency planning, and the willingness to turn around. No operator can guarantee a summit—and anyone who does is not being honest.

Why are your prices lower than some competitors?

We work directly with vetted local guide companies instead of layering on unnecessary middlemen and a large corporate overhead. That can create better value, but it does not mean cutting safety, fair pay, park fees, or essential logistics. We will always show you what is included and explain any price difference clearly.

Can I book a private expedition or bring my own group?

Yes. Private departures are a strong fit for families, friends, training partners, and climbers who want more control over dates or pace. We can build the itinerary, local team, and logistics around your group’s objectives.

Do I need travel insurance?

For international, high-altitude, or technical travel, comprehensive insurance with medical evacuation coverage is strongly recommended and may be required for some itineraries. We will tell you what coverage is appropriate for your route; read the policy’s altitude and activity exclusions carefully.

Will I meet my guide before the expedition?

You will have clear pre-trip communication with Outland and know the guide-team plan before departure. Depending on the objective, we can also arrange a pre-departure video conversation or introduce you during the final planning stage. Once in-country, your local team handles the mountain directly.

What happens if I need to change dates or cancel?

Policies depend on the local operator, permits, lodging, and how close the trip is. We will give you the written terms before you book and help explore practical options if plans change. Travel insurance can be particularly valuable here.

Can I add a safari, cultural day, or extra travel to my trip?

Often, yes. A safari after Kilimanjaro or a few days around a mountain town can add depth to the trip when it is planned with enough recovery and buffer time. Tell us what matters to you, and we will build it into a realistic itinerary.
